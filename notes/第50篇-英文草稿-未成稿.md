The Dutch East India Company kept a **gazetteer** of every island where **nutmeg** grew. **Groves** outside it were **felled**. Each nut shipped west was **coated** in **chalk** so it could flavour an Amsterdam dish but never **germinate** in a rival's **soil**. In 1667 the company gave Manhattan to England for Run, the one Banda island its **directory** still **skipped**.

The **cartel** rested on **scarcity**, and that rested on the count being right. Ten pounds of the **spice** cost less than a penny in Banda and fetched two pounds in Europe. Every guard, every **warehouse**, every burnt **grove** existed to hold that gap open. The precautions were sound against smuggled seed, which was the theft the company had imagined.

Pierre Poivre, **intendant** of Mauritius, wanted **saplings**. In May 1769 he **despatched** an **agent**, Provost, with two ships, **spades**, and orders to **dig**. At Miao the party found **stumps**: that grove had been cut years before. A **disaffected** Dutchman rescued the voyage. He hated his masters and knew the islands the **compilation** never held. He named Gueby.

Provost sailed in 1770 with 400 **seedlings** and seventy **clove** trees, roots packed in earth. They were **transplanted** on Mauritius, and from that **estate** went on to Zanzibar, Madagascar, Grenada. The **climate** suited them. They **flourished**.

**Owning** a living thing means **owning** a list of places. The company held its **dominion** for a century and a half and defended every name on it. But the list was **ragged**, and stayed so because the company remained **unaware** of it — nothing inside a count can report what the count has left out.
