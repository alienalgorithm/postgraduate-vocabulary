In 1974 one stray female salmon was found stuck on a power-station intake screen on the tidal Thames,
the first in 140 years, and the river was declared cured. Sewage treatment and oxygen barges had done it.

It had not come home. From 1975 the Thames was stocked from Scottish hatcheries: 60,000 to 80,000
smolt a year released into tributaries. About one per cent returned. Through the 1980s the count
rose, to over 300 in the early 1990s, every one tagged. No wild spawning was ever recorded. After
1995 the run fell; in 2005 none were recorded.

Genetic analysis (Griffiths 2011): against a baseline of 3,830 fish, all 16 untagged salmon caught
2005-2008 originated not from the stocked fish but from other rivers in southern England. Strays.

Thirty years of stocking founded nothing. What swims up the Thames came on its own. You can count
what returns; you cannot count, from that, whether the river made it.
