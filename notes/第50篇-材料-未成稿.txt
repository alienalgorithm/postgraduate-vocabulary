The Dutch East India Company kept a register of every island where nutmeg grew. Groves outside the
register were cut down. Every kernel exported was rolled in lime so it could flavour a dish in Amsterdam
but never sprout in a rival's soil. In 1667 the company gave Manhattan to England for Run, the one Banda
island still outside its ledger.

The precautions were sound. They were sound against smuggled seed, which was the theft the company had
imagined.

Pierre Poivre, intendant of Mauritius, wanted saplings, not kernels. In May 1769 he despatched a clerk,
Provost, with two vessels and orders to dig. At Miao the party found stumps: the company had felled that
grove years before. A disaffected Dutchman rescued the voyage. He hated his employer and he knew the
islands the register omitted. He named Gueby.

Provost sailed in 1770 with 400 nutmeg saplings and seventy rooted clove trees. They reached Mauritius,
then Zanzibar, Madagascar, Grenada.

Ownership of a living thing is ownership of an inventory of places. The company guarded every entry for
a century and a half. It never learned how incomplete the inventory was.
