The honeybee is the American insect: on the seal of Utah, the state insect of a dozen more, in every child's book of the farm, the **queen** and the **drone**, the **honey** in the cupboard, the **beeswax** on the table, the **honeycomb**, the **nectar**, the **sting** and the **stinger** left behind. Nothing seems more native to a meadow than a bee.

It came by ship. The Virginia Company's fleet that reached **Jamestown** in April 1622 carried **hives** below deck, two years after the Mayflower; by 1639 escaped **swarms** were nesting in the **Massachusetts** **forests**. The **colonists** wanted honey, their only **sweetener**, and wax, because tallow **candles** stank, and **mead**. Pollinating was not on the list; nobody valued it until the end of the nineteenth century.

The bees went west faster than the **wagon-train**. Half a hive leaves every spring and flies to a **hollow-tree**; each **bee-tree** was a day's flight further out, and the **bee-hunter** followed the **bee-line** to it with an axe from his **log-cabin**. The Indians, Jefferson wrote, called the honeybee the white man's fly, because where it appeared the settlers came a season behind. It still took 231 years to reach the Pacific — **Oregon** in 1853, by sea again.

The continent was not empty of bees. It had the **bumblebee**, the **wasp** and the **hornet** and hundreds of lone bees, and the **Maya** had kept stingless bees for a thousand years. It had even had honeybees once: a **paleontologist** found a worker in **Miocene** **shale** in **Nevada**, fourteen million years old. The **genus** died out here long before it sailed back.

Today the white man's fly still moves ahead of the crop. **Bee-keeping** is not a **hobbyist**'s trade but a haulage one: every February most of the country's **box-hives** go down the **interstate** on a **flatbed** **semi-trailer**, stacked on **pallets**, to **pollinate** the **almonds** of California, then on to the **cherries**, the **alfalfa** and the **buckwheat**, a **migratory** herd. The bee is not native to the meadow. It is native to the road.
