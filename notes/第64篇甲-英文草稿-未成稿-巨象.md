The story the **showman** told was this: on the night of 15 September 1885, at St Thomas, Ontario, Jumbo saw the **locomotive** coming, **scooped** the **dwarf** elephant Tom Thumb up in his **proboscis**, **flung** him clear, and charged the engine head-on, knocking it off the rails and dying a hero of the **menagerie**.

The newspapers told another. After the show the two were being walked along the **siding** to their **boxcar** when an unscheduled freight came round the curve. Tom Thumb was caught on the front of the engine and thrown down the bank with a broken leg. Jumbo, walking behind, was shut between the bank and the circus train with nowhere to go, and was hit from the rear. He died in minutes with his **attendant** Matthew Scott beside him. The engine did leave the rails, but not because an **elephant** charged it.

Then the showman went to work on the body. The **pelt** went to Ward's in **Rochester**, where the **taxidermist** Carl Akeley pulled it over a shape taller than the animal had stood: Barnum had billed his **giant** at thirteen feet; he was ten feet seven. The bones went one way and the **taxidermy** another, each drawing its own crowd. The heart was sold to Cornell. The **grease** was **bottled** as **ointment** for **rheumatism**. The **stomach** yielded English pennies, **keys**, **rivets** and a police whistle.

The stuffed **behemoth** **toured** with the circus for two years, then went to Tufts, whose **campus** made him its emblem; the pelt burned in 1975 and the **ashes** sit in a peanut-butter **jar**. The **tusks** and **ivory** are in New York.

Alive he drew a crowd; dead he drew several. The last thing the showman stuffed was the story of how he died.
